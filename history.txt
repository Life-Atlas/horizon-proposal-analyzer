WINNIIO's reference delivery is a paid, fixed-price, milestone-gated engagement (2026, ongoing) for a certified European steel manufacturer (~40 employees, EUR 10-15M turnover; name withheld under customer confidentiality). The customer planned ~45 concurrent, penalty-bearing projects across 10 work centres in a 46-worksheet Excel estate (a dozen-plus interlinked formula families) maintained personally by the managing director 10-15 hours/week — with late-delivery penalties of ~2% of contract value per week, contractor crews on three-week mobilisation lead times, and progress data fragmented across four disconnected systems. The planning physics parallel a turnaround: fixed windows, compounding overrun cost, scarce crews, fragmented truth, key-person dependency. Delivered and demonstrated live to the customer's CEO: the spreadsheet estate reverse-engineered and digitised, with duration norms mined from history rather than assumed; a live planning dashboard on real production data fed by two live enterprise API integrations (MES REST API live 25 June 2026; HR/time-system OData API live 17-24 June 2026), nightly automated syncs with failure alerting and an audit log, source ontology reconciled with the customer's CFO; weekly demand versus crew capacity per work centre honouring mobilisation lead times; a schedule-risk and penalty-exposure board with criticality tiers; and Gantt/traffic-light progress monitoring on daily refresh. Gap quantification and live demand-vs-capacity re-planning were demonstrated 6 July 2026; prescriptive recommended actions per at-risk project are a committed pilot deliverable. On WINNIIO's five-rung capability ladder (answers, shows, tells, prescribes, acts): rungs 1-2 delivered; rung 3 demonstrated as risk-tiered dashboard flagging; rung 4 partially evidenced (gap quantification); both earned fully in the pilot; rung 5 roadmap. Status stated honestly: a live system on real production data, demonstrated to the customer's CEO (24 June and 6 July 2026 — demonstration video available on request) and confirmed by the CEO in writing 7 July 2026 ("all of the feedback has been implemented... looks very good"), with production acceptance testing 17 July 2026 — a directional reference, not a measured post-deployment result. Because this proof is adjacent-industry rather than oil and gas, the proposed ADNOC entry point is de-risked by design: a back-tested pilot on ADNOC's own completed turnaround at fixed price, with an honest-negative clause and full artefact handover in open formats, before any autonomy is discussed.
